# Optical Signatures of Structural Failure Regimes in Immiscible Fluids

## Subtitle

A Structural Flow Prediction and Testing Protocol

## What is this?

This is a scientific prediction and testing protocol. It proposes that structural failure regimes in immiscible fluids should leave distinguishable optical signatures when light is used as a probe.

It does not report completed laboratory results. It is a proposed test.

## Why should I care?

The artifact turns a Structural Flow claim into something ordinary science can test, support, weaken, or falsify. It asks whether independently classified fluid regions produce distinguishable families of optical behavior.

## What is the claim?

The central prediction is:

failure regime → fluid structural condition → light-probe optical profile → measurable optical variables → falsifier

The fluid region must be classified first. The light-probe result is tested second. A region should not be called Shear because a laser speckles; it should be classified from the fluid behavior first, and only then tested optically.

## What is this not?

This is not a completed experiment, not a replacement for fluid mechanics, optics, electromagnetism, photon theory, quantum electrodynamics, or General Relativity, and not a claim that Structural Flow replaces ordinary science.

## How was this done?

The protocol translates five Structural Flow failure regimes — Dissolution, Shear, Drift, Echo, and Lock — into immiscible-fluid conditions and predicted optical profile families. It then maps those profiles to ordinary optical measurements such as boundary contrast, scattering, intensity, phase, beam displacement, recurrence, relaxation time, speckle contrast, and hysteresis.
